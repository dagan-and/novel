# 07. Legal Tax Realism Rules

Updated: 2026-07-20

## Main Rule

This canon must stay on the clean side:

- Pay taxes.
- Keep records.
- Use tax professionals.
- Do not frame actions as evasion.
- Do not give or use "how to avoid suspicion" tactics.

Yongsik's quiet life comes from explanation, not concealment.

## Stock Taxes In Early Korea Setting

Domestic listed stocks:

- For ordinary small shareholders in the 2004-style setting, domestic listed stock capital gains are generally treated as simple.
- Securities transaction tax is handled at sale.
- Dividend tax is withheld.
- The story does not need Yongsik to file complex domestic stock capital gains during the early small-shareholder phase.

US stocks:

- More complex.
- Need trade statements.
- Buy/sell prices.
- FX rates.
- Fees.
- Dividends and withholding.
- Annual reporting may be needed.

Story approach:

- Early teen years focus on Korean stocks.
- Later, when wealth is large, accountants handle filings.

## Mother Account And Gifts

Early trading account:

- Mother's name is used for simplicity in the 2004 beginning.

But:

- If large assets move to Yongsik, legal/gift issues must be handled.
- Mother's assets are legally hers if under her name.
- Later transfers must be treated as gifts or properly documented.

Mother's 5 Suji apartments:

- They are not borrowed-name assets.
- They are a real gift to mother or bought for her with proper tax.

Monthly support:

- From 2015, 10 million KRW per month to mother.
- If it exceeds ordinary support and becomes wealth transfer, gift tax should be handled.

## Relatives

If Yongsik gives younger relatives a Seoul home:

- Example: 1 billion KRW home.
- Must be handled as:
  - Gift contract.
  - Gift tax.
  - Acquisition tax.
  - Source-of-funds documentation.

Rule:

- One-time housing support is okay.
- Endless living-cost support to many relatives creates family conflict and suspicion.
- It is better to set internal family rules.

Possible rule:

- Marriage housing support once.
- Maximum support per person.
- Taxes paid by Yongsik.
- No repeated unlimited cash.

## Cash And Gold

Earlier idea:

- Convert remaining money into gold or 50,000 KRW notes and put it in a safe.

Final realism:

- Do not use this as a concealment method.
- Large unexplained cash/gold is risky.
- It breaks clean records.
- Later use creates source-of-funds problems.

Allowed flavor:

- Small symbolic private safe:
  - Several hundred million KRW in cash.
  - Some gold bars.
  - Important documents.
- Main wealth remains in recorded accounts, bonds, deposits, brokerages, and real estate.

## Japan Marriage And Nationality

Marriage to a Japanese woman:

- Does not automatically make Yongsik Japanese.
- He remains Korean.
- To live in Japan, he can use a spouse-of-Japanese-national residence status or similar legal route.
- Later permanent residency or naturalization may be considered, but there is no need for the story unless desired.

## Japan Bank Account

Yongsik can plausibly open Japanese bank accounts if:

- He legally resides in Japan.
- Has residence card/status.
- Has Japanese address registration.
- Meets bank requirements.

Use:

- Japan living expenses.
- House costs.
- Car costs.
- Child expenses.
- Misaki's monthly money.

Do not frame as:

- "Move money to Japan so nobody notices."

Large foreign accounts can trigger reporting obligations depending on tax residency and law.

## Korea/Japan Tax Residency

Since Yongsik lives between Korea and Japan:

- Tax residency should be handled by professionals.
- Korea assets remain reported in Korea.
- Japan living money is sent legally.
- If Japan tax residency applies, Japanese filings may be needed.
- Overseas financial account reporting can matter.

Canon practical solution:

- Korean accountant.
- Japanese tax professional.
- Clean remittance records.
- No hidden accounts.

## Quiet Rich Strategy

Bad strategy:

- Suspicious cash withdrawals.
- Hidden gold.
- "Spent it" records while actually hiding it.
- Borrowed-name assets.
- Tax avoidance.

Good strategy:

- Pay tax.
- Keep receipts.
- Use legal gifts.
- Use recorded financial products.
- Keep a public occupation:
  - Personal investor.
  - Asset manager.
  - Real estate manager.
- Spend openly but not like a celebrity.
- Stay out of media.

## Spending And Visibility

Spending can reduce cash buildup, but it does not erase tax reality.

Allowed:

- Hotels.
- Luxury restaurants.
- Cars.
- Travel.
- Gifts with tax handled where relevant.

Risk:

- Very large gifts to girlfriends can create gift-tax issues.
- Frequent huge public purchases create rumor risk.

Canon solution:

- Bachelor period has heavy spending, but after 2014 he remains mostly explainable.
- After marriage, spending becomes quieter and family-centered.
