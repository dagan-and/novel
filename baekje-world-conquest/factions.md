# Factions

## Baekje-Mahan Union

- Status: dominant regional power
- Strengths: centralized reform, rising food output, strong long-term planning
- Weaknesses: aristocratic resistance, limited industrial base, reform fatigue risk
- Goal: secure the peninsula, control nearby seas, then project power onto the continent

## Peninsula Rivals

- Status: fragmented or resisting incorporation
- Strengths: local legitimacy, terrain familiarity, regional loyalties
- Weaknesses: weaker administration, less efficient mobilization
- Role: immediate consolidation target

## Wa Polities

- Status: divided maritime powers across the sea
- Strengths: naval mobility, local alliances, fragmented leadership exploitable by outsiders
- Weaknesses: internal rivalry, uneven military organization
- Role: potential client states, allies, or future annexation targets

## Liaodong and Northern Frontier Powers

- Status: gateway to continental expansion
- Strengths: cavalry traditions, strategic depth, border resilience
- Weaknesses: unstable alliances, dependence on frontier momentum
- Role: first major test of overseas and overland imperial expansion

## Chinese Continental States

- Status: richest long-term prize and greatest structural challenge
- Strengths: population, bureaucracy, urban centers, production capacity
- Weaknesses: factional politics, regional warlordism, court instability
- Role: eventual decisive theater for universal empire
