# Turn 009

## Strategic Posture

Cheonin slows the advance to digest the bridgehead. This frustrates younger commanders, but he insists that empires die from winning too fast as often as from losing.

## Major Actions

- roads, depots, clinics, and tax offices are established in the Liaodong corridor
- local notables are split into collaborators, hostages, and exiles
- iron plows and protected seed grain are distributed to cooperative settlements
- propaganda frames Baekje rule as harsh, ordered, and materially superior

## Outcomes

The first occupied territories begin feeding the occupation rather than draining it. Some locals rebel, but others choose the conqueror who repairs wells and punishes bandits. This is the true revolution of Cheonin’s empire: it does not merely defeat states, it embarrasses them by governing better.

## State Changes

- occupation efficiency improves
- local collaboration rises in selected areas
- ideological confidence inside Baekje peaks
- reform bureaucracy becomes indispensable

## Risks Next Turn

- success may hide brewing resentment among conquered elites
- central dependence on Cheonin’s guidance grows dangerously deep
