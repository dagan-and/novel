# Turn 006

## Strategic Posture

Cheonin pivots back to the peninsula to crush remaining resistance before the northern war. The message is clear: no rear threat, no divided sovereignty, no exceptions.

## Major Actions

- integrated army columns move against holdout mountain and river strongholds
- roads built for reform are now used to move grain, ladders, and siege timber
- captured elites are offered office in exchange for hostages and tax compliance
- rebel villages that surrender early are spared to encourage collapse by example

## Outcomes

Resistance breaks faster than expected because Baekje’s real weapon is not courage but continuity. It can attack, withdraw, and return with food, tools, and written orders while its enemies exhaust themselves in a single stand. By year’s end, the peninsula is effectively under one military system.

## State Changes

- peninsula control becomes near total
- revolt risk falls in frontier districts
- siege competence improves
- hostage politics expands central leverage

## Risks Next Turn

- forced integration of local elites may create hidden traitors
- the army is becoming powerful enough to threaten the throne if badly managed
