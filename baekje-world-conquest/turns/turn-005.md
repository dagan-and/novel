# Turn 005

## Strategic Posture

The First Sea Campaign begins, not as open annexation but as controlled intervention. Cheonin wants a client order in Wa before any mainland empire can use the islands against Baekje.

## Major Actions

- a royal-backed claimant in Wa receives ships, grain, armor, and advisors
- Baekje marines seize two harbor settlements used by hostile factions
- military engineers demonstrate shock tactics with shield walls, signal drums, and timed landings
- local collaborators are promised trade privileges rather than immediate subjugation

## Outcomes

The campaign is brief and brutal. Wa chiefs expecting a raid instead face a disciplined amphibious force that lands, fortifies, and resupplies before they can unite. Baekje does not rule the islands directly, but now decides who can plausibly become king.

## State Changes

- maritime prestige surges
- a friendly Wa bloc emerges
- naval logistics prove viable
- treasury strain increases from overseas operations

## Risks Next Turn

- client rulers may submit only while Baekje fleets remain nearby
- sudden success may deepen court factional rivalry over spoils
