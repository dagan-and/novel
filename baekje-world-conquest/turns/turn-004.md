# Turn 004

## Strategic Posture

Cheonin turns outward without invading. Merchants, envoys, and spies are sent toward Wa and the northern frontier to measure weakness before choosing a target.

## Major Actions

- trade ships carry iron tools, salt, lacquer, and grain to Wa intermediaries
- agents map rival lineages across the sea and identify factions open to Baekje backing
- northern scouts chart roads, passes, and river crossings toward Liaodong
- court scribes produce the first comparative census of manpower and taxable households

## Outcomes

Wa appears divided enough to be manipulated without full conquest. The northern frontier, by contrast, looks dangerous but fragmented. Cheonin concludes that the sea should be secured first so the rear remains quiet when the continental war begins.

## State Changes

- intelligence quality improves
- diplomatic leverage over Wa increases
- long-range planning becomes more precise
- the court gains confidence in phased expansion

## Risks Next Turn

- foreign agents may notice Baekje preparations
- a delayed strike north could give frontier rivals time to consolidate
